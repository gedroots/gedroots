title: Privacy and network use
description: What stays on your computer, the few cases where GEDRoots goes online, and how living people are protected.
---

Family trees hold personal data about people who never chose to be in them. GEDRoots is built so that your research stays on your computer unless you decide otherwise.

## What stays on your computer

- Your `.ged` file and your media. GEDRoots has no account, no server and no cloud storage.
- Your settings: those of the tree are in the `.ged` file; those of the app (recent files, AI agent setting) are in your system's settings folder.
- No analytics, no telemetry.

## When GEDRoots goes online

GEDRoots connects to the internet only for updates and for these features, each started by something you do:

| Feature | Connects to | Sends |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Updates](/get-started/install#updates), at launch and twice a day unless you uncheck **Automatic Updates** | GitHub, where GEDRoots is published | Nothing about you or your tree: it asks for the list of versions and downloads the new one |
| Map | OpenStreetMap tile servers | The map area you're viewing |
| Place suggestions while typing, **Locate** | [Photon](https://photon.komoot.io), an OpenStreetMap search service | The place name you typed |
| **Search On**, web links, **GEDCOM for Geneanet** | The website, in your browser | What the website's address contains, such as the person's name |
| **Help › Report an Issue…** | GitHub, in your browser | Your description and, if **Include Context** is checked, the context below. Nothing is published until you submit the issue on GitHub |

Map tiles are kept on disk once downloaded. Switch the map to **Offline** to stop any connection from it; in that mode, GEDRoots shows only the tiles it already has.

## Reporting an issue

**Help › Report an Issue…** opens a new GitHub issue with your description. **Include Context** (checked by default, **Show** displays it) adds what helps reproduce the problem, and nothing from your tree:

- the GEDRoots version, the system (name, version, processor, memory, preferred languages), the interface language and appearance, the window size;
- the screen shown (such as “Individuals › Tree”), the type of the selected record, whether a search is active (not what it searches);
- the number of records of each type, places and media files, the GEDCOM version, the program that wrote the file, the file size and the names of the extension tags it uses (such as `_UID`);
- the update and AI agent settings;
- the last commands you ran and the last errors, by name (such as `AddEvent`, `ws.save_failed`), without their message;
- after a crash, the place in GEDRoots' code where it happened.

It is written as JSON, so that it can be read by scripts. No name, date, place, identifier or file path is included. GitHub issues are public: in the description, describe the people with made-up names.

## AI agents

When you [connect an AI agent](/workflows/ai-agents), GEDRoots runs a server that only your own computer can reach: it listens on `127.0.0.1`, requires a secret token, and refuses requests from web pages. The setting is off until you turn it on.

What the agent reads may be sent to its provider, depending on the agent you use. GEDRoots limits what it sees:

- **Living people are reduced to what identifies them.** A person with no death, burial or cremation is treated as living, unless their birth, or a date that places it (their own events, a union, a child's birth, a parent's birth), is more than 100 years ago. Of a living person, an AI agent only gets what identifies them: their name, their birth (date and place) and their close relatives, never their other events or notes.
- **The agent can't write.** It can only propose records. You review, compare and save them yourself.

## Living people in exports

Exports and the `.ged` file itself contain everyone in your tree. Before you share a file or publish on Geneanet, make sure the living people in it agree, or remove them from the copy you share.
